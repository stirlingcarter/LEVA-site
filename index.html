The Problem

Standard practice methods are statistically inefficient.

Observations:

  • Fixed curricula can't adapt to individual weakness topology
  • Most practice time is spent on already-mastered material
  • No quantitative feedback loop for competency gaps

We measure response latency across a 200-dimensional symbol space and schedule accordingly.

Musical proficiency is measurable as a high-dimensional latency distribution. What if practice sequences targeted the statistical boundary between fluent and not-yet-fluent?

Jazz piano isn't one skill—it's hundreds of chord voicings, each with distinct motor requirements and recall latency across twelve keys.

Competency topology is irregular. Fast at major 7ths, slow at half-diminished. Comfortable with drop-2 voicings, uncertain on rootless shapes.

LEVA maps this in real-time and generates practice sequences that target the boundary between mastered and not-yet-mastered material.

We have the data to show this works.

System Architecture

01 — The Skill Tree

Progressive Unlocking

Voicings are organized into domains—Major 7, Dominant 7, Altered Dominants, Quartal, etc. Advanced material remains locked until foundational competency is demonstrated.

Working memory constraints limit effective symbol set size. The skill tree maintains practice at the edge of current ability.

Locked content unlocks as competency thresholds are met

02 — The Probability Engine

Weighted Selection

Each practice attempt updates the latency model. Symbol selection is drawn from a weighted probability distribution:

  • Latency weight — Higher latency increases probability
  • Recency penalty — Recent items suppressed
  • Circle of fifths adjacency — Musical coherence
  • Register diversity — Prevents position stagnation
  • Volatility smoothing — Maintains flow state
  • Forgetting curve modeling — Scheduled reappearance
  • Key signature distribution — Coverage across all keys
  • Transition pair frequency — (Corpus analysis in progress)

Appears random to the user. Optimizes for competency gaps.

03 — The Human Element

Self-Assessment Protocol

The system asks the user to judge adequacy of execution, rather than imposing algorithmic thresholds.

Accommodates individual standards

Technical Foundation

The scheduling algorithm combines eight weighted scoring functions: latency gap, recency penalty, volatility, cluster cohesion, circle-of-fifths proximity, diatonic compatibility, latent similarity, and data scarcity indicators.

Selection is drawn from a softmax probability distribution. The interface presents a single question: "Was that adequate?"

"The goal is competence, not engagement metrics."

Preliminary Findings

Longitudinal session data reveals patterns in voicing acquisition. Initial observations:

Pareto Distribution Observed

Approximately 20% of voicings account for 80% of practice time. High-latency items correlate with confusion adjacency (similar voicings) rather than objective difficulty.

→ Clustering confusable voicings together forces disambiguation.

Transfer Effects Quantified

Mastery of drop-2 major 7 correlates with ~30% reduction in learning time for drop-3 major 7. Voicing families share underlying motor patterns.

→ Skill tree sequence has measurable impact on learning efficiency.

Session Duration Effects

Latency improvement peaks around 25-30 minutes, plateaus thereafter. Some evidence of regression beyond 45 minutes, suggesting motor fatigue.

→ System recommends breaks after 30 minutes.

Intended Users

🎹
Intermediate Pianist

3-5 years of study. Comfortable with chord symbols and lead sheets, but lacking comprehensive voicing vocabulary for convincing comping.

Develops mechanical fluency to allow focus on musical expression rather than shape-hunting.

🎷
Multi-Instrumentalist

Primary instrument elsewhere, seeking functional piano comping ability. Theoretical understanding present, motor patterns underdeveloped.

Assumes harmonic literacy. Targets execution, not conceptual learning.

🎓
Educator/Researcher

Teaching jazz piano. Interested in quantitative assessment of student weakness patterns and skill transfer dynamics.

Provides data-driven insights for targeted lesson planning.

Scope Limitations:

  • ❌ Requires existing chord symbol literacy
  • ❌ Does not teach music theory or harmonic concepts
  • ❌ Not designed for passive or distracted practice
