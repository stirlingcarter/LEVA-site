"Where Your Practice Time Actually Goes"

What if your practice routine could see the exact shape of your incompetence— and systematically fill it in?

Jazz piano proficiency isn't one skill—it's a 200-dimensional point cloud of chord voicings, each with its own latency metric across all twelve keys.

You're fast at major 7ths. Slow at half-diminished. You nail drop-2 voicings but stumble on rootless shapes. Your brain has a complex, lumpy topology of what it knows.

Three Layers of Intelligence

01 — The Skill Tree

Progressive Mastery,
Not Chaos

Voicings are organized into domains—Major 7, Dominant 7, Altered Dominants, Quartal, etc. Advanced voicings stay hidden until you've got the fundamentals.

Your working memory can't handle 200 symbols at once. The skill tree keeps you at the edge of your ability, not drowning.

Foundation Tier: ├─ Major 7 (drop 2, drop 3, rootless) ├─ Minor 7 (drop 2, drop 3, rootless) ├─ Dominant 7 (drop 2, drop 3, rootless) └─ Half-diminished Intermediate Tier: ├─ Dominant alterations (♭9, ♯9, ♭13) ├─ Major 6/9 voicings ├─ Sus voicings └─ Upper structure triads Advanced Tier: ├─ Quartal voicings ├─ Cluster voicings ├─ Polychords └─ "Wrong note" substitutions Meta Tier: ├─ ii-V-I progressions (all keys) ├─ Turnarounds ├─ Rhythm changes └─ Modal interchange
02 — The Probability Engine

Weighted Randomness
That Feels Like Flow

Every time you play, LEVA updates its latency model. The next symbol isn't random—it's drawn from a weighted probability distribution:

  • Latency weight — Slow voicings appear more
  • Recency penalty — No mindless repetition
  • Circle of fifths adjacency — Musical logic
  • Register diversity — No hand position stagnation
  • Success rate smoothing — Flow state
  • Forgetting curve modeling — Old material resurfaces
  • Key signature distribution — All keys get hit
  • Transition pair frequency — (Coming: Real Book corpus)

Feels random. Optimizes for your gaps.

What We're Learning

Every session generates data about how people actually learn voicings. Some patterns emerging:

80/20 Rule Holds

~20% of voicings account for 80% of practice time. Not the "hardest" ones—the ones with highest confusion adjacency (voicings that look/sound similar).

→ Cluster confusable voicings together to force disambiguation.

TODO: Bar chart

Transfer Learning is Real

Drop-2 major 7 mastery accelerates drop-3 major 7 learning by ~30%. Voicing families share motor patterns.

→ Unlocking sequence matters. Some voicings are "gateway skills."

Session Sweet Spot

Latency improvement peaks around 25-30 minutes, then plateaus or regresses. Motor learning fatigue is real.

→ Suggest breaks after 30 min. No marathon sessions.
